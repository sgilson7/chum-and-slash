## The tool

A third-person sci-fi hack and slash for the browser, played from a page on GitHub Pages, with combat after Devil May Cry 5 and Dragon's Dogma 2. A player on a desktop or laptop fights with keyboard and mouse, or with a PS5 or Xbox controller plugged in, with no set-up. The MVP is the prologue's first three arenas, with their tutorials and checkpoints, and a sandbox where the player spawns enemies and fights them.

What Sam asked for, as Sam read the transcript (from the opening message, kept so nothing is dropped; the transcript above governs where they differ):

- A third-person, sci-fi hack and slash in the style of Devil May Cry 5 and Dragon's Dogma 2. Combos that feel like button mashing but aren't. Abilities on L1 plus a face button.
- The story setting: a stable colony city of about a million people behind a wall. Native fauna slow anything that moves fast near them, so bullets fail and everyone fights hand to hand with a glowing utility tool (axe, pick and blade). The defence force has fallen, and the prologue is the evacuation.
- The hero: a black shirt, cuffed corduroy trousers striped white and brown about every centimetre, a green coat, and a bladed tool.
- Controls: WASD and lock-on (it can be released for freestyle), with light, heavy, launcher and jump. Keyboard and mouse, and a PS5 or Xbox controller that works when plugged in. Haptics for light, heavy and launcher hits, from small to large.
- Rules: stamina is spent only by sprinting, moves in the air and abilities; enemies take 50% more damage in the air; launchers work only on stunned enemies; every enemy has idle, stunned and airborne states, run by behaviour trees; combo caps are light 4 or 5 in a row, heavy 3, interleaved 6, and after a cap comes a recovery; the first combos are light, heavy, light, light, heavy and that string ending in a launcher; attacks move the attacker forward, and hits push enemies back.
- The MVP is the first three arenas, plus a sandbox where you spawn enemies and fight them. Arena 1: one dog; dogs strafe at range, then lunge to bite; one hit stuns them. Arena 2: three dogs on a tutorial tree that attack together; the game slows and asks for the Whirlwind Strike, which stuns all three. Arena 3: a badly injured large dog that does not fight back, for practising the launcher combo; the combo kills it.
- Checkpoints just before each fight, and skippable cutscenes.
- Arenas are parks on hilltops, or along a lake with the city across the water, with vistas like Dark Souls 3's Ringed City. Draw the distance in rings like Chumworld Arena. Long Time Comin' and Pursuer buildings are fine as distant city. The fauna are brightly coloured, long-limbed and plant-like.
- A guiding rule: the player can always tell a hit landed, through animation (stagger, or a shake when there is no stagger), hit-stop, sound (different for a hit and a miss) and haptics.
- Tutorials teach through play: the game slows, pauses, asks for a button, and the button does its move.

## Done means

Each is checked by a test, a probe or the browser check, named in `PLAN.md`.

- The page opens from its GitHub Pages address in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, makes no off-origin request, and runs at a steady frame rate on a laptop without a graphics card (a lower draw scale on a software renderer).
- Keyboard and mouse play the whole MVP. A PS5 or Xbox controller, plugged in, plays it with no set-up, and rumbles small for a light hit, more for a heavy hit and most for a launcher hit where the browser allows rumble.
- Every landed hit shows in four ways: the enemy's reaction (stagger, or a shake when it does not stagger), hit-stop, a hit sound that differs from the miss sound, and a rumble. A core test fails if a hit omits any of them.
- The combat rules in "What Sam asked for" each hold by a core test: stamina, air damage, launcher on stun only, the three enemy states, the combo caps and recovery, the first two combos, forward motion and push-back.
- Enemies run on behaviour trees ported from Vagrancy, with a parity test against Vagrancy's own.
- Arena 1, Arena 2 and Arena 3 play as described, each starting from a checkpoint just before its fight, with skippable cutscenes. A scripted bot clears each arena in a core test.
- The sandbox spawns dogs and the large dog on demand.
- The tutorials slow, pause and prompt for a button; pressing it performs the move.
- A playtester subagent, given only the text harness, says the combat feels great, and the journal has the round-by-round record that led there.
- The design journal explains the moving parts as animations drawn from the core's or harness's own output.

## Accessibility

Hits are confirmed by motion, hit-stop, sound and rumble together, so no one channel is needed alone. The page carries state without colour alone: shapes and text for lock-on, stamina and prompts. Tutorial prompts name the button for the device in use (keyboard, PlayStation or Xbox glyph text). Input: keyboard and mouse, and standard-mapped gamepads (PS5 DualSense, Xbox). Prompts wait for the player and never time out. The page honours `prefers-reduced-motion` for camera shake.

## Style

Protection of the Spheres' 3D style: characters and creatures built from primitives (spheres, prisms, cylinders), animated by keyframes. Sci-fi colony; bright, long-limbed, plant-like fauna. Not Blender's realistic characters.
